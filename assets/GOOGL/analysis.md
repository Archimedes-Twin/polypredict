## Drivers

The earnings engine is the main driver. Revenue and operating income grew strongly in the latest quarter, and the dip in operating cash flow traces to a European fine and a build in TPU inventory that the evidence says returns cash next year, so it reads as accounting noise and not business decline. The selloff after that report was about capex sentiment, not earnings quality, which makes it a sentiment-driven de-rating. Rates matter less than the headlines suggest. Alphabet is a net-cash megacap, so interest income on its cash offsets the interest cost of data-center debt, and rising long yields should be roughly neutral for it. A further source of upside is the private portfolio, including stakes in SpaceX, Anthropic, Waymo and others, which the share price does not appear to reflect. Khoo's intrinsic-value estimate now sits well above the market price, whereas earlier in the year the stock traded above it. That swing shows how much of the recent move is repricing of sentiment and how little is a change in business value. No market-derived signals are attached to this asset, so the macro read rests on the evidence alone.

## Continue or revert

The call is bullish with low confidence. There is no raw market prior for GOOGL, so the posterior comes entirely from the evidence, and no market odds are available to confirm or contradict it. The evidence points toward continuation of the earnings engine and a reversion of price toward intrinsic value as capex panic fades. The caution is timing. The stock has broken below a key support level, and even Khoo says he would add only at the next lower support or on a bullish reversal pattern, so the valuation gap does not guarantee an immediate bounce. That tension is why confidence stays low despite the bullish lean. The call weakens if capex spending keeps climbing without a visible path to returns, if cash flow fails to recover as the TPU inventory converts, or if earnings growth slows so that the selloff becomes fundamental and not sentimental. A decisive further break lower with no reversal pattern, or a yield shock large enough to break the net-neutral rate argument, would also argue for stepping back from the bullish stance.

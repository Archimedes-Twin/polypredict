## Performance (6 months)

Over the six-month window QQQ ended modestly lower, but the path was a round trip, not a steady slide. It climbed to a window high in early autumn, then gave those gains back in a sharp, fast unwind into early November. The most recent month did nearly all the damage. The fund now sits close to its window low with very little bounce, so the shape reads as a failed breakout followed by rapid liquidation.

## Drivers

Rates are the main macro driver. Futures price a strong likelihood of a Fed hike at the December meeting, which pushes real yields higher and compresses the long-duration growth multiples that dominate the index. Oil and broader geopolitics are secondary. Policy risk around AI is the second layer.

The latest sharp one-day drop was tied to Democratic rhetoric about an AI and data-center moratorium. The video commentary calls it a sentiment shock, not a fundamental break, and argues that the Senate math and a likely veto make passage unlikely. Earnings are the counterweight. Profit growth is strong, guidance is rising, and hyperscaler negative free cash flow looks like growth capex rather than weaker operating cash flow.

Against that, AI leaders remain far below their highs, money is rotating toward financials, healthcare and staples, and crowded hedge-fund longs are being cut. That flow argues for near-term underperformance. The dot-com valuation and boom-duration comparisons support long-run upside but say little about the next three months.

## Continue or revert

The call is very bearish with high confidence, and the market odds agree with the down trend. The crowd expects continuation, mainly because of the hawkish rate path. The evidence-adjusted posterior has not been computed for this run, so the market-implied reading is the operative view. I am not claiming any softening from the video evidence.

The video evidence pulls the other way. It argues for a policy-driven dip with intact earnings and a V-shaped rebound. I weight the market odds more heavily. The Fed path is a concrete, currently priced force on valuations. The rebound thesis depends on political outcomes and historical analogies that have not yet shown up in price. Several of the bullish videos are also older or long-horizon, so they say little about a quarter-length window. The rotation and degrossing evidence is a flow effect that fits this horizon.

The call would be invalidated if hike expectations fell decisively toward a coin flip, which would remove the main valuation headwind. It would also be invalidated if the moratorium threat faded and AI and semiconductor leaders began recovering in price. Further escalation in rate expectations, or a credible legislative path for the moratorium, would deepen the bearish case.

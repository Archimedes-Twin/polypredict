## Performance (6 months)

Over the six months QQQ ended modestly lower, but the path was a wide round trip rather than a steady slide. It bottomed in late summer, climbed to a window high in early October, then gave back the gains in a sharp, fast unwind through early November. The latest month did nearly all the damage. The drawdown from the October peak was steep, and the fund sits almost on its window low with barely any bounce. The shape is a failed breakout followed by rapid liquidation, and the trend reads as down.

## Drivers

Rates are the main macro driver. Futures price a high likelihood of a Fed hike by the December meeting, a hawkish turn that pressures the long-duration growth multiples dominating the index. Policy and geopolitics add a second layer. Khoo attributes the latest one-day plunge to Democratic rhetoric about an AI and data-center moratorium, which he calls a sentiment shock rather than a fundamental break. He argues the Senate math makes passage unlikely, since a moratorium would need a supermajority and a presidential veto would require even more to override. Earnings are the main counterweight: he points to strong third-quarter growth, rising fourth-quarter guidance, and hyperscaler revenue growth, with negative free cash flow explained by growth capex and one-offs rather than weaker operating cash flow. On the other side, AI names sit far below their highs, money is rotating into financials, healthcare and staples, and crowded hedge-fund longs are being degrossed. That unwind argues for continued near-term underperformance. The valuation and boom-duration comparisons to the dotcom era support long-run upside but say little about the near term.

## Continue or revert

The call is bearish: the evidence-augmented posterior is less bearish than the raw market prior, but it stays on the downside. The odds agree with the down trend, and the crowd expects continuation, driven mainly by the hawkish rate path. Khoo's evidence fights that, arguing the selloff is a policy and sentiment shock with intact earnings that should rebound quickly, as in 2018 and 2025. That is why the posterior softens the prior rather than matching it. It does not flip the call, because the rate pressure is a concrete macro force and the crowding unwind is still in progress, while the rebound thesis depends on political outcomes and historical analogies that have not yet shown up in price. Continuation of the decline is therefore the better judgment, with a shallower slope than the market implies. The call would change if Fed hike odds fell decisively below the midpoint, which would remove the main valuation headwind. It would also change if the moratorium threat visibly faded and AI and semiconductor leaders began to recover, confirming the V-shaped rebound view. A further escalation in rate expectations or a credible path for the moratorium would deepen the bearish case.

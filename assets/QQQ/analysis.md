## Performance (6 months)

Over six months QQQ ended modestly lower, but the path was a wide round trip rather than a steady slide. It climbed to a window high in early autumn and then gave those gains back in a sharp, fast unwind into early November. The latest month did most of the damage. The fund now sits close to its window low with almost no bounce, so the shape reads as a failed breakout followed by rapid liquidation.

## Drivers

Rates are the main macro driver. Futures price a high likelihood of a Fed hike at the December meeting. That hawkish turn pushes real yields up and squeezes the long-duration growth multiples that dominate the index. Oil and broader geopolitics are secondary here, and policy risk around AI is the second layer.

The latest one-day plunge was tied to Democratic rhetoric about an AI and data-center moratorium. Khoo calls it a sentiment shock rather than a fundamental break. He argues the Senate math makes passage unlikely, since it would need a supermajority and would face a presidential veto. Earnings are the counterweight: strong profit growth, rising guidance, and hyperscaler negative free cash flow explained by growth capex rather than weaker operating cash flow.

Against that, AI leaders remain far below their highs, money is rotating into financials, healthcare and staples, and crowded hedge-fund longs are being degrossed. That flow argues for near-term underperformance. The dot-com valuation and boom-duration comparisons support long-run upside but say little about the next three months.

## Continue or revert

The call is bearish. The evidence-adjusted view is less bearish than the raw market-implied reading, but it stays on the downside. Market odds agree with the down trend, so the crowd expects continuation, mainly because of the hawkish rate path.

The video evidence pulls the other way. It argues for a policy-driven dip with intact earnings and a quick V-shaped rebound. I weight the market odds more heavily. The Fed path is a concrete, currently priced force on valuations, while the rebound thesis rests on political outcomes and historical analogies that have not yet appeared in price. Several of the bullish video claims are also older and long-horizon, so they carry less weight over ninety days. The evidence softens the slope of the expected decline but does not flip the direction.

The call would be invalidated if hike expectations fell decisively toward the midpoint, removing the main valuation headwind. It would also be invalidated if the moratorium threat faded and AI and semiconductor leaders began to recover in price. Further escalation in rate expectations, or a credible legislative path for the moratorium, would deepen the bearish case.

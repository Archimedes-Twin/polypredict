## Performance (6 months)

IBIT's window was a round trip that ended with a net loss. It sold off early and bottomed at the end of June. A sizable rally followed into a late-summer high in September, and the shares have since given back nearly all of that advance. The latest leg down is the sharpest stretch of the whole window. Price now sits just above the June trough, close to the bottom of its range. The overall shape is a failed recovery: a rebound that could not hold, then a fast unwind. The path was choppy and wide-ranging, with a clear downward lean at the end and a deep peak-to-trough drawdown.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the dominant driver. Markets price a rate hike at the December meeting as close to a certainty, which keeps real yields elevated and financial conditions tight. That is a hostile backdrop for a liquidity-sensitive asset with no earnings or cash flow to anchor its valuation. Duration-like and speculative assets tend to be hit hardest when the policy debate shifts from cuts to hikes.

Commentary in the evidence set points the same way. One investor said he may agree with the view that Bitcoin ultimately goes to zero, framing it as a mania-chasing vehicle with no cash flows. A more recent argument questions how durable the leaders in AI are, given that frontier-model leadership keeps changing hands, switching costs are low, and growth at the top names is slowing while valuations assume dominance. That is not a Bitcoin-specific point. It does suggest that appetite for richly priced, narrative-driven growth exposure is fragile, which is the pool of risk capital IBIT draws on.

## Continue or revert

The market prior and the posterior are both firmly bearish, so there is no prior-versus-evidence conflict to resolve. The crowd already expects the downtrend to continue. The video evidence is bearish too, though it adds only modest weight. The July remark has decayed with age and is a generic valuation critique rather than a catalyst. The November point is more recent but only indirectly related to Bitcoin. The evidence therefore confirms the prior rather than overturning it, and the macro hike pricing carries most of the case.

The call is that the downtrend continues. The main risk is a relief bounce, since price is already near the June low after a steep drop. The view would be invalidated by a sharp repricing of Fed hike expectations, with December hike odds falling well below even, and by a reclaim of the $49 September high, which would show the failed recovery was not failed after all. Absent either, a break below the $33 June low is the more natural next test.

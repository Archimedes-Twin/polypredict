## Performance (6 months)

Over the past half-year IBIT went sideways in a very wide range, with a small net loss. It bottomed at the end of June, rallied hard into a late-September high, then gave back a large share of that gain. The last month has been the sharpest leg down. The drawdown from the peak is deep, and the fund now sits in the upper-middle of its range rather than at either extreme. The shape is a failed breakout, not a clean uptrend or downtrend.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the main macro driver. Markets price a high chance of a rate hike at the December meeting, and only about even odds of any cut by the July meeting of next year. That keeps real yields and financial conditions restrictive, which is a poor backdrop for an asset with no earnings or cash flow and a dependence on liquidity. The one evidence item is Khoo's early-July comment that he may agree with Grantham that Bitcoin goes to zero. It is an opinion on a mania-prone, non-earning asset, not a read on flows, and its weight has faded with age. It still leans the same way as the rate signals, so it is a modest sentiment tilt and not an independent catalyst.

## Continue or revert

The call is bearish. The raw market prior is neutral with low confidence, and its probability of a rise sits just under even. The odds alone give no directional edge. The evidence-adjusted posterior moves slightly lower and raises confidence to medium, because the restrictive rate signals and the skeptical stance on a cash-flow-free asset point the same way. The conflict is small. Nothing in the evidence argues for a rebound, and the odds do not argue against caution, so the sell-off from the September high is more likely to continue than to fully revert. The posterior is only modestly below the prior, which means conviction is moderate. The call would change if December hike pricing faded materially, or if the odds of a cut by the July meeting broke lower. Either would revise the rate signals and take away the tightening argument. Fresh, stronger evidence supportive of Bitcoin flows would also challenge the view.

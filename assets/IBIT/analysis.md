## Performance (6 months)

IBIT's six months were a round trip with a net loss. It sold off early and bottomed around the end of June. A strong rally followed into a late-summer high, and the shares have since given back most of that advance. The latest leg down has been the sharpest stretch of the whole window, and price now sits near the bottom of its range rather than the top. The shape is a failed recovery: a rebound that could not hold, followed by a fast unwind. It is choppy and wide-ranging, with a clear downward lean at the end.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the dominant driver. Markets price a hike at the December meeting as close to a certainty, which keeps real yields high and financial conditions tight. That is a hostile backdrop for a liquidity-sensitive asset with no earnings or cash flow to anchor its valuation. Hawkish pricing like this usually reflects inflation that has not cooled enough for the Fed to ease.

Cut expectations for mid-next-year are roughly a coin flip. That offers only a mild offset, because it implies a long stretch of restrictive policy before any relief.

The qualitative evidence adds little weight. Adam Khoo's July remark that he may agree with Grantham's call for Bitcoin to go to zero has largely decayed. His later skepticism about the durability of AI leadership is a sentiment signal about the risk-appetite complex, not a direct read on Bitcoin. Both point the same way, but they are small contributions next to the rate backdrop.

## Continue or revert

Continue. The very bearish rating stands. The market prior already leans clearly bearish, and the decayed video evidence nudges it slightly lower. The prior and the evidence agree, so there is no conflict to resolve. The prior carries most of the weight because it rests on a concrete, near-certain Fed hike, while the evidence is one stale opinion and one tangential critique.

The split odds on cuts by mid-next-year soften the picture slightly but do not overturn the hike signal. The call would revise if hike odds fell decisively below their invalidation level, meaning the tightening path had genuinely eased. A sharp drop in the odds of cuts by mid-next-year would deepen the bearish case instead. Until the Fed signals a shift, further weakness is more likely than a durable recovery, and rebounds look like rallies to fade rather than reversals.

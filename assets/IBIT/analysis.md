## Performance (6 months)

IBIT swung through a wide range over the half-year and ended with a modest net loss. It bottomed at the end of June, rallied hard into a late-September high, and has since given back a meaningful part of that advance. The latest month was the sharpest leg down. Price now sits in the upper part of the range rather than at either extreme, so the shape reads as a failed breakout, not a clean trend.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the dominant macro driver. The market prices a very high likelihood of a hike at the December meeting, which keeps real yields elevated and financial conditions restrictive. That is a poor backdrop for a liquidity-sensitive asset with no earnings or cash flow to anchor its valuation. Inflation and yield pressure sit behind the hike pricing, and they are the channel through which the macro regime weighs on the asset.

The video evidence points the same way. In early July, Adam Khoo said he may agree with Grantham that Bitcoin goes to zero, and he treats it as a speculative, mania-chasing vehicle. The remark is old and its weight has decayed, so it adds a valuation-skeptic voice but little new information.

## Continue or revert

The call is very bearish with high conviction, and the macro mechanism favors weakness persisting while the Fed stays on a tightening path. The video item barely moves the view from the market-implied starting point. It agrees with the crowd's pricing and does not fight it, so there is no conflict to resolve. I weight the market odds more heavily because they reflect live Fed pricing, while the video is a stale, lone opinion. Conviction is high because the inputs agree, not because any single piece of evidence is strong.

The June rebound coincided with a window when the rate headwind looked less certain, and the later giveback fits a restrictive-rate regime reasserting itself. The main thing that would change the view is a repricing of the Fed. If the Fed-hike odds at the December meeting fell decisively, the rate headwind would ease and the call would be revised toward neutral or better. Fresh, credible evidence that flows or risk appetite were overriding tight policy would also matter. Absent either, the posterior stays firmly bearish.

## Performance (6 months)

SCHD has climbed in a steady, mostly orderly uptrend, from a spring trough in early May to a peak in early November. The latest close sits just under that high, so the fund trades near the top of its range instead of bouncing off the bottom. The most recent month was the strongest stretch of the window, a sharp acceleration after a slower grind higher. The drawdown from the peak is shallow, and the path reads as persistent accumulation, not a spike followed by a retreat.

## Drivers

The main driver is rotation out of AI-heavy growth and into the sleeves SCHD owns: consumer staples, healthcare, energy and utilities. October's sector tape showed all four leading, which is the clearest evidence for the call. Adam Khoo's framework says this rotation arrives in stages, out of AI, back in, then out again over a long stretch, so value catch-up should repeat rather than end abruptly. His July note that he was buying profitable non-AI dividend compounders almost daily shows the same positioning. The policy backdrop is hawkish, since the market assigns a high probability to a Fed hike at the December meeting. That would normally weigh on yield-oriented equities. But the rate path is also pushing investors away from long-duration, richly valued growth and toward cash-generating businesses with real earnings, which is the profile SCHD holds. Energy strength adds to the inflation-sensitive tilt of the basket.

## Continue or revert

The evidence-augmented posterior sits above the raw market prior, and both are very bullish with high confidence. The crowd and the evidence agree with the six-month trend, so the call is continuation, not reversion. The one tension is the elevated hike pricing, which is a textbook headwind for dividend funds. The sector tape and the staged-rotation thesis outweigh it, because the same hawkish path is what drives money out of AI growth and into value. The evidence therefore adds conviction to the odds instead of fighting them. The call would change if hike odds fell well below the current majority, which would revise that signal and remove the macro force behind the rotation. It would also change if the sector leadership in staples, healthcare, energy and utilities faded and money moved back into AI in a way that breaks the staged pattern. A deep drawdown from the early November high would be a further warning that accumulation has turned into distribution.

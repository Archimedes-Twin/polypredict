## Performance (6 months)

Over the last six months SCHD has climbed in a steady, mostly orderly uptrend, rising from a spring trough in early May to a peak in early November. The latest close sits just under that high, so the fund is trading at the top of its range rather than bouncing off the bottom. The most recent month was the strongest stretch of the window, a sharp acceleration after a more gradual grind higher. The drawdown from the peak is shallow and has been largely recovered, which makes the path look like persistent accumulation rather than a spike followed by a retreat.

## Drivers

The main driver is sector rotation out of AI-heavy growth and into the fund's core sleeves of consumer staples, healthcare, energy and utilities. October's sector tape showed those groups leading, and Adam Khoo's read is that the rotation arrives in stages, out of AI, back in, and out again over one to two years, so the catch-up in value and dividend baskets repeats instead of ending in one move. His July stance of buying profitable non-AI dividend compounders points the same way. Rates are the main counterweight. The market prices a meaningful chance of a Fed hike by the December meeting, which reflects sticky inflation and would normally pressure yield-sensitive equities. Markets also give roughly even odds of a cut by the July 2027 meeting, so the expected path is a high plateau followed by eventual easing, not a runaway tightening cycle. Cash-generative staples, healthcare and energy holdings are less exposed to higher discount rates than long-duration growth, and an inflation-linked rate path with energy supply risk tends to favor those same sleeves.

## Continue or revert

The call is bullish, with medium confidence. The raw market prior sits in the neutral zone with low confidence, because the Fed odds say little about the direction of a dividend fund over the next quarter. The evidence is what lifts the posterior above that prior. The rotation flows are visible in the October sector data, and the staged-rotation thesis implies the move continues rather than reverts. Evidence from one commentator is an opinion about flows, not a fact, so the upgrade is measured and stops short of high conviction. Hawkish rate pricing is the conflict, and it is already largely reflected in the prior. It is not enough to override flows that favor the fund's exact sectors. The call would change if Fed hike odds for the December meeting fell materially, or if odds of a cut by July 2027 faded well below even. Either would signal that the rate backdrop that has supported the energy and defensive value sleeves is changing. It would also weaken if rotation reversed and money flowed back into AI-led growth, since the fund's recent leadership depends on that flow continuing.

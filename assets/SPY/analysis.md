## Performance (6 months)

Over the last six months SPY has moved sideways inside a broad range rather than trending. It bottomed in early summer, climbed steadily to a peak in early October, and then gave back part of that advance in a sharp, short pullback over the most recent month. The drawdown from the high is modest, well short of a deep correction, and the index still sits in the upper half of its range with a small net gain over the half-year. The record shows a choppy base with a late-stage dip, not a breakdown.

## Drivers

The main driver is the earnings cycle. Third-quarter profits beat an already high bar, and growth is broadening beyond the mega-cap technology names, with margins at a high level that the evidence attributes to real AI cost savings. Because earnings have outgrown price, the forward multiple has slipped below its five- and ten-year averages and is well under where it started the year, which limits valuation risk. Long yields sit near their historical norm, so the argument is that the level of rates is not a structural threat as long as earnings keep growing and the rise in yields is not abrupt. Flow and seasonal evidence also leans supportive: hedge funds have just made one of their largest forced de-grossings in a decade, a pattern that has historically lined up with market lows, and post-midterm periods have historically been strong once election uncertainty clears. These are historical regularities, not guarantees. The offsetting risk is geopolitical. The Iran ceasefire-hold market prices low odds of durability, which feeds an energy and risk-premium shock channel and is the main reason the market prior leans bearish.

## Continue or revert

The call is bullish, but with low confidence. The raw market prior is bearish because it is dominated by the Iran ceasefire proxy, which prices a fragile truce and a likely return of geopolitical stress. The evidence fights that reading on several fronts: earnings breadth and margins are strong, valuation has cheapened, selling pressure looks exhausted after the de-grossing, and the post-midterm seasonal turns favorable. We resolve the conflict in favor of the evidence, treating the pullback as a continuation of the broader range with an upward tilt rather than the start of a reversal, while keeping conviction low because the geopolitical risk is real and unpriced by the earnings data. The odds are not wrong, they are incomplete. What would change the call: ceasefire-hold odds recovering well above the midpoint would remove the bearish drag and strengthen the bullish view. Conversely, a break below the floor level named in the evidence, roughly the depth of a full correction from the highs, or a renewed escalation that pushes energy and yields sharply higher would show the earnings and flow support is being overwhelmed, and the call would move back toward the market prior.

## Performance (6 months)

SPY has traded sideways rather than trending. It climbed unevenly through the summer on strong earnings, then stalled and gave back part of the advance in a sharp early-November selloff. That selloff was triggered by an AI data-centre policy shock and compounded by forced deleveraging. The drawdown is modest next to a true correction, and the index remains in the upper part of its range. The shape is a choppy, wide base with a late air-pocket, not a breakdown and not a clean uptrend.

## Drivers

Earnings are the anchor. Profits beat a high bar, growth is broadening beyond mega-cap tech, and margins reflect real AI cost savings. Because earnings have outpaced price, the forward multiple sits below its long-run averages. Long-term yields sit near historical norms, so the risk lies in how fast they rise, not in their level. The Fed path and energy-driven inflation remain the swing factors for that speed. Tech demand cuts both ways: AI capex supports growth, while data-centre restrictions threaten it. Credit risk shows up as leverage, with hedge-fund and margin-funded retail unwinds creating mechanical selling.

## Continue or revert

The judgment is neutral, with low confidence. The video evidence leans bullish, but the market odds give no directional edge, and I side with the odds. The evidence comes from one commentator, and many points restate the same seasonal and base-rate arguments. The freshest items also contradict each other, one calling for a quick V-shaped rebound and the other warning of more forced selling. Earnings strength argues against a deep break, but nothing justifies a strong call. A reversal of the AI policy shock, a calm bond market, or contained energy prices would favor upside. Earnings damage, a disorderly rise in long yields, or an energy spike would favor downside.

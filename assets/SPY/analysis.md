## Performance (6 months)

Over the half-year SPY has moved sideways inside a broad range rather than trending. It bottomed in early summer and climbed unevenly to a peak in early October. It then gave back part of that advance in a sharp pullback over the most recent month. The drawdown from the high is modest and well short of a deep correction. The index still sits in the upper half of its range with only a small net gain, so the record shows a choppy base with a late dip rather than a breakdown.

## Drivers

The main driver is the earnings cycle. Quarterly profits beat an already high bar, and growth is broadening beyond the mega-cap technology names. Margins are elevated, and the evidence attributes that to real AI-related cost savings. Earnings have outgrown price, so the forward multiple has slipped below its long-run averages and well under its level at the start of the year. That leaves less valuation risk and some room for earnings-led upside.

Positioning and seasonality add support. Hedge-fund de-grossing was historically large, and past episodes of that scale have tended to coincide with market lows. The post-midterm period has also been favorable historically. The main risk is geopolitical. Market odds that the US-Iran ceasefire holds are low, which signals an energy and risk-premium shock. That view would soften if ceasefire-hold odds recovered decisively. Leveraged retail and fund flows could also cause a few more days of forced selling.

## Continue or revert

The market-implied view leans bearish, while the video evidence leans bullish, and the posterior lands on the bullish side. I weight the evidence more heavily here, for three reasons. The odds come from one narrow geopolitical proxy, which is an indirect read on equities. The evidence speaks directly to earnings, valuation and positioning. And the selloff looks like a sentiment and deleveraging shock rather than an earnings shock, a type that has tended to recover quickly.

Conviction stays low, because the geopolitical signal has not been refuted and the mechanical selling could extend before it exhausts. The base case is that the recent dip reverts and the index retests its highs. A sustained escalation in the Middle East, especially one that lifts oil sharply, would invalidate that view and favor the bearish read.

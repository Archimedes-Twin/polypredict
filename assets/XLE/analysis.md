## Performance (6 months)

Energy has been the standout sector of the last six months — a steady, choppy uptrend that outran the broad market, with sharp pullbacks along the way tied to oil-price headlines rather than any break in the trend. The shape is a rising channel: higher lows, occasional violent dips that got bought. Over the past stretch, though, the advance has flattened and the recent action has been two-way rather than one-directional.

## Drivers

The sector's case rests on physical oil markets rather than sentiment: supply discipline and geopolitical risk premium keep crude floors firm, while demand worries and OPEC supply decisions cap upside. Macro-wise, a Fed that is not hiking takes pressure off the dollar and supports commodities, but any re-acceleration of inflation would help energy as a hedge while hurting the broad market — the sector tends to do well exactly when the macro picture turns stagflationary. The watchpoint is crude breaking down toward the low end of its recent range: a decisive slide through the WTI ninety-dollar invalidation would undercut the supply-tightness story that has carried the sector.

## Continue or revert

This is a genuine standoff, so the honest call is to sit it out rather than push either way. The market-odds side leans bearish — the odds basket has been pricing more downside than upside for the sector. The video-evidence side leans mildly bullish: recent rotation commentary treated energy as a top destination and tied rising rates to a commodity revenue tailwind, though that evidence is aging and partly decayed, and the presenter himself avoids the sector long-term as too volatile. Neither side is strong enough to overturn the other, so continuation of the flat, two-way posture is favored over a new trend in either direction. A recovery in the oil-floor odds would tip the balance bullish; a confirmed break of the crude invalidation would tip it bearish — until one of those happens, reverting or doubling down are both unjustified.

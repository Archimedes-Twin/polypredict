## Performance (6 months)

XLE climbed off a midsummer trough at the start of July and trended higher through the autumn, peaking at the very end of October. It then gave back part of that gain, and the most recent month has been essentially flat. The advance was a staged grind with a real pullback from the high, not a straight line. The upward shape is intact but has paused just below the peak.

## Drivers

Geopolitics is the main driver. The market gives the US-Iran ceasefire a low chance of lasting through year-end, so oil carries a supply risk premium that supports energy equities. The macro backdrop adds to this. In mid-September Adam Khoo pointed out that rising rates alongside rising inflation have been a good regime for energy and commodity stocks this year, because inflation lifts commodity revenue. His early-November note recorded energy as a top rotation destination in October, so fund inflows now sit on top of the oil supply bid. Khoo also treats the sector as volatile and unpredictable and avoids it for the long run. That is a warning about swings, not a bearish call on direction.

## Continue or revert

Continue. The evidence-adjusted posterior is bullish with high confidence and sits a little above the raw market prior, which is also bullish. The two agree, and the evidence adds to the crowd's lean. Inflation-driven rate pressure and rotation inflows both point the same way as the geopolitical risk premium, and none of the evidence argues for reversal. Khoo's long-run caution about the sector's volatility is why a pullback like the one since the late-October high should be expected, not read as a trend break. What would change the call is the Iran signal. If ceasefire-hold odds recover to a clear majority, the supply premium would fade and the bullish view would need revising. A sustained slide in crude, or a reversal in the rotation flows that have supported the sector, would also weaken it.

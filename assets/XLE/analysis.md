## Performance (6 months)

Energy rose over six months from a midsummer low to a peak at the very end of October, then gave back part of the gain. The last month was roughly flat, so the upward shape is intact but has paused after a meaningful pullback from the high.

## Drivers

Geopolitics is the main driver. Low odds that the US-Iran ceasefire holds imply a supply risk premium in oil, while the market also prices a dip in crude, so the signals pull in both directions. Rising inflation and rates have historically been a tailwind for commodity revenue, and Khoo notes inflows as energy became a rotation destination. He also regards the sector as volatile and unpredictable and avoids it long-run.

## Continue or revert

Judgment: neutral. The raw market-prior view was also neutral, and the evidence confirms it. Rotation and the inflation regime are supportive, but the oil-dip odds and a volatile geopolitical backdrop offset them. The view would be revisited if the odds of a sub-90-dollar WTI dip fall below the revision threshold shown on this page, which would remove the downside oil signal. It would also be revisited if ceasefire-hold odds recover above the revision threshold shown on this page, which would reduce the supply premium.

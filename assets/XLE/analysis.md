## Performance (6 months)

XLE climbed off a midsummer trough at the start of July and trended higher through the autumn, peaking at the very end of October. It then gave back part of that gain, and the latest month has been slightly negative and mostly sideways. The advance was a staged grind with a real pullback from the high, not a straight line. The drawdown from the peak is moderate and looks like consolidation rather than a break in structure, and the price now sits well above the summer low but short of the high.

## Drivers

Geopolitics is the main driver. The market assigns a low chance that the US-Iran ceasefire holds through year-end, so oil keeps a supply risk premium that flows into energy earnings and equities. The macro backdrop reinforces this. In mid-September Adam Khoo noted that rising rates alongside rising inflation have been a good regime for energy and commodity stocks this year, because inflation lifts commodity revenue. His April point that the broad index was being held up by energy while quality growth names fell shows the earnings effect of elevated oil. His early-November note recorded energy as a top rotation destination in October, which adds a flow bid on top of the supply bid. He still calls this a bizarro year and avoids the sector over the long run as volatile, so the regime may not persist.

## Continue or revert

The call is continuation, and the posterior sits above the raw market prior. The crowd already leans bullish because ceasefire durability is priced as unlikely. The evidence agrees with that and adds two independent supports: an inflation-and-rates regime that favors commodity revenue, and rotation inflow into the sector. Nothing in the evidence fights the odds. The only tension is Khoo's warning that the regime is unusual and the sector volatile, and that is why this is a lean toward continuation, not a certainty. The flat latest month and the pullback from the October high look like digestion of a strong move, not a reversal. The call would change if ceasefire-hold odds recovered decisively into majority territory, since that would remove the supply premium that anchors the thesis. It would also change if the rate-and-inflation pairing broke down or the October rotation into energy reversed into outflows. Any of those would pull the posterior back toward, or below, the market prior.

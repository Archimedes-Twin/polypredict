## Performance (6 months)

XLC has moved lower across the window, with a drawdown that is deep relative to its earlier peak. The decline reads as a grind lower punctuated by a sharper leg down late in the period, not a single shock. The fund set its high early in the window and its low very near the end, so it is still pressed close to the lows. Bounces have been shallow and have not rebuilt momentum. There is no sign yet of a base or a durable recovery.

## Drivers

The Fed is the central driver. Markets price a hike, not a cut, at the final meeting of the year. A higher policy path lifts real yields and the discount rate on long-duration growth equities, and a handful of mega-cap platforms dominate this sector, so rate sensitivity is concentrated. Sticky inflation keeps policy restrictive and leaves little room for relief from the labor market or from easier financial conditions.

Sector specifics add pressure. Communication services was the weakest sector in the latest month as capital rotated out, and Meta and Alphabet fell sharply from their highs on capex-sentiment worries. The spending is growth capex, and the cash-flow drags look tied to one-offs. Even so, markets tend to penalize the outlay before the payoff shows up. Agentic AI shopping and search tools are a slower structural threat to ad-funded platforms, because agents may route purchases around display advertising and intermediaries.

## Continue or revert

Continue lower. That fits the very bearish, high-confidence posterior. The raw market prior is already strongly bearish, and the decayed video evidence mostly reinforces it rather than fighting it. The short-term outflow call and the agentic ad-erosion call both point down. The ad-erosion call carries a long half-life, so it still counts. The one conflicting piece argues that the selloff is sentiment, not fundamentals, and that growth capex will convert into earnings while the one-off cash drags reverse. I resolve that conflict in favor of the prior. The fundamental argument works on a horizon of quarters, while the current window is governed by the rate path and by observed selling pressure. The conflicting evidence is therefore a reason to revisit the call once hike risk fades, not a reason to fade the trend now.

The bearish read would be invalidated if hike odds for the December meeting dropped well below the threshold the signal names. A decisive reversal in sector flows would also force a reassessment, because the fundamental case would then no longer be fighting the rate path.

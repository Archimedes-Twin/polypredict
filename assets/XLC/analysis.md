## Performance (6 months)

XLC has trended lower over the window. It peaked in mid-spring and then slid in a mostly steady grind, with no sustained recovery attempt. The decline sharpened in October, when communication services was the worst-performing sector in the market, and the low came in the final trading days of that month. The bounce off the low has been modest, and the latest month still closed in the red. The fund sits well below its spring high, and the drawdown looks like persistent distribution rather than a single shock.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the central macro driver. The market is pricing a hike, not a cut, by the final meeting of the year. That raises discount rates on long-duration growth equities, which describes the mega-cap names that dominate this sector. The sector's content and media businesses also have weak pricing power, so they have little cushion against tighter financial conditions.

October added sector-specific pressure. Rotation outflows and a capex-sentiment panic hit Meta and Alphabet hard. The counterargument is that this is growth capex: Alphabet showed strong revenue and operating income growth, Meta's operating income held up once a one-off fine is excluded, and the negative free cash flow traces to one-offs whose cash returns later. That suggests the drop is sentiment rather than fundamentals, but it plays out over quarters, not within this window.

The structural evidence leans the other way. Consumer AI agents that make purchases on behalf of humans threaten ad-funded platforms, because display ads lose value when an agent does the buying. Non-diversified ad platforms face the sharpest disintermediation risk, which weakens the longer-term advertising story.

## Continue or revert

The call is continuation lower, consistent with a very bearish rating. The market odds and the six-month trend agree, and the evidence-adjusted posterior stays in the same bearish territory as the raw odds. The video evidence is mixed: two items point down and one points up. It does not overturn the odds-based view.

I weight the rate-path odds and the observed outflow more heavily than the fundamentals defense. The fundamentals argument is credible, but it works on a longer horizon than this window. The Fed-hike leg and the structural pressure on advertising act on the current window directly.

The call would change if hike odds for the December meeting fell well below the invalidation level, which would remove the discount-rate pressure on long-duration growth. It would also change if earnings kept confirming that capex converts into growth while the sentiment panic faded, which would turn the sentiment-versus-fundamentals gap into a rebound case. I would revisit the stance early next year, when the delayed cash returns arrive and hike risk may roll off. Until then, the path of least resistance stays down.

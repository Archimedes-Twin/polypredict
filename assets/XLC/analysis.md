## Performance (6 months)

XLC has trended lower over the half-year. It peaked in early May, then slid in a mostly steady grind with no sharp V-shaped recovery attempts. The decline accelerated in October, when communication services was the worst sector in the market, and the low came at the very end of that month. The bounce off that trough has been modest, and the last month still closed lower. The fund sits well below its spring high, and the drawdown is deep and reflects persistent distribution rather than a single shock.

## Drivers

Rates and the earnings narrative around mega-cap AI spending are the main drivers. The market is pricing a Fed that hikes rather than cuts by the December meeting. That raises discount rates on long-duration growth stocks, and the sector's biggest constituents are exactly that kind of stock. On top of that, October saw a rotation outflow and a capex-sentiment panic that pushed Meta and Alphabet far below their highs. The counter-evidence is real: Alphabet is growing revenue and operating income strongly, Meta's operating income is up once a one-off fine is excluded, and the negative free cash flow comes from one-offs such as the EU fine and TPU inventory whose cash returns later. That points to a sentiment problem, not a fundamental one. A third, slower-burning risk is agentic disintermediation. Consumer AI agents that route purchases away from humans could erode advertising revenue at ad-funded platforms, since display ads are worth little to an agent. This is a forward-looking opinion rather than a demonstrated earnings hit, but it gives bears a structural argument.

## Continue or revert

The call is very bearish, one notch more negative than the raw market prior, which is already bearish. Here the odds and the evidence mostly agree. The crowd expects the downtrend to continue, and the evidence adds to that rather than fighting it. The one bullish item, that capex is growth capex and the sell-off is sentiment, is a fair long-run argument. But it describes where fundamentals will land, not what flows and discount rates will do in the near term. With rotation outflows ongoing and hike expectations lifting yields, sentiment-driven de-rating can outlast the earnings rebuttal. The extra weight beyond the market prior comes from the disintermediation thesis and the October outflow. The call is to expect continuation, not reversion. The main thing that would change it is a collapse in Fed hike odds, which would remove the rate headwind and revise the view. A second trigger would be earnings that visibly show the capex converting into cash flow, which would turn the sentiment-versus-fundamentals gap into a recovery.

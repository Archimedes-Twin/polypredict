## Performance (6 months)

XLC has trended lower across the six-month window. It peaked in mid-May and then slid in a mostly steady grind, with no sustained recovery attempt along the way. The decline sharpened in October, when communication services was the worst sector in the market, and the trough landed in the final trading days of that month. The bounce off the low has been modest, and the latest month still closed in the red. The fund sits well below its spring high. The drawdown is deep and reads as persistent distribution rather than a single shock.

## Drivers

The Fed path is the central macro driver. The market is pricing a hike, not a cut, by the final meeting of the year. That lifts discount rates on long-duration growth equities, and the mega-cap names that dominate this sector fit that description. The October evidence adds sector-specific pressure. Rotation outflows and a capex-sentiment panic hit Meta and Alphabet hard and left both far below their highs. A counterpoint came at month-end: the capex is growth capex, Alphabet reported strong revenue and operating income growth, Meta's operating income held up once a one-off fine is excluded, and the negative free cash flow traces to one-offs such as the EU fine and TPU inventory whose cash returns later. That supports the view that the drop is sentiment rather than fundamentals, but it is a slower-acting offset. The heavier evidence is structural. Consumer AI agents that route purchases away from humans threaten ad-funded platforms, because display ads lose value when an agent does the buying. Middlemen and non-diversified ad platforms face the sharpest disintermediation risk, which weakens the longer-term advertising story.

## Continue or revert

The call is continuation lower, consistent with a very bearish rating. The evidence-augmented posterior sits slightly below the raw market prior, so the evidence adds a small bearish tilt to what the odds already say. The one bullish item, the earnings-backed defense of growth capex, argues the selloff is overdone on fundamentals. It does not outweigh a rate path pricing a hike and an agentic-disintermediation threat to advertising revenue, so it does not move the call. The trend and the odds agree, which strengthens the case for continuation over reversion. The call would change if hike odds for the December meeting fell well below the invalidation level, which would remove the discount-rate pressure on long-duration growth. It would also change if earnings kept confirming that capex is converting into growth while the sentiment panic faded, since that would turn the sentiment-versus-fundamentals gap into a rebound case. Absent either, the path of least resistance stays down.

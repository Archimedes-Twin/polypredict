## Performance (6 months)

Gold has had a hard half-year. It peaked in mid-spring, then slid in an orderly, persistent way into a trough at the very end of October. That was a deep drawdown rather than a sudden crash. The recovery since the low has been shallow, and the price sits nearer the bottom of its range than the top. The latest month has been flat to slightly softer, so the direction is still down and no clear base has formed.

## Drivers

The dominant headwind is the Fed path. The market treats a hike at the year-end meeting as the likelier outcome. That keeps real yields firm and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. It is the clearest macro explanation for the slide from the spring high, and it is the only signal driving the call.

No recent video evidence is on file for gold, so the commentary layer neither reinforces nor challenges the rate-driven story. The main offsetting force is geopolitics. Renewed conflict could revive safe-haven demand and energy-led inflation worries, but the market is not currently pricing that into the Fed path.

## Continue or revert

The evidence-adjusted posterior matches the raw market view, because there is no video evidence to pull it away from the odds. The crowd expects continuation and the half-year trend agrees, so there is no conflict to resolve. I weight the market odds because they are the only live input, and they point the same way as price action.

The call is very bearish with high confidence. The slide is more likely to continue than revert while a year-end hike remains the base case. The view would change if hike odds fell decisively below the midpoint, since that would ease the pressure from real yields and open the door to a rebound from depressed levels. A sharp geopolitical shock that overrides rate expectations would be a second reason to reassess. Absent either, the downtrend and the pricing point the same way.

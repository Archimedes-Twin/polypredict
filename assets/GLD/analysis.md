## Performance (6 months)

Gold has had a hard six months. It peaked in mid-May and slid in an orderly, persistent way into a trough at the end of October, a deep drawdown rather than a single crash. Since that low the recovery has been shallow, and the price sits close to the bottom of the range. The most recent month has been flat to slightly softer, so the trend is still downward and no clear base has formed.

## Drivers

The main headwind is the Fed path. The market treats a hike at the December meeting as the likelier outcome. That keeps real yields firm and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset, which is the clearest macro explanation for the slide from the May high. The offsetting force is geopolitics. The market gives only modest odds that the US-Iran ceasefire holds through year-end, so renewed conflict is a live risk that could bring safe-haven and energy-driven inflation demand back into gold. These two forces pull in opposite directions, and neither dominates. No recent Adam Khoo evidence is available to tilt the balance, so the drivers rest on the market-implied signals alone.

## Continue or revert

The evidence-augmented posterior and the raw market prior agree on a neutral, low-conviction stance, and the posterior sits only fractionally above the prior. With no evidence items to contribute, there is no real conflict between evidence and odds to resolve. The hawkish Fed pricing argues for continued weakness, and the fragile ceasefire argues for a rebound. Together they leave no strong three-month edge, so the call is balanced and the realized path could go either way. The call would shift toward a rebound if hike odds fell well below even, since that would ease the real-yield pressure. It would shift the same way if ceasefire-hold odds recovered to a clear majority, which would reprice the geopolitical backdrop. Hike odds staying elevated while the ceasefire stays doubtful would keep the balance unresolved, with the downtrend still intact.

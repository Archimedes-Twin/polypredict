## Performance (6 months)

Gold has had a hard half-year. It peaked in mid-May and then slid steadily, bottoming out at the end of October. That is a deep, orderly drawdown rather than a single crash. Since the low, the price has barely recovered and sits close to the trough, so the pullback has been working lower rather than snapping back. The recent month has been flat to slightly softer, and the broader trend remains clearly downward.

## Drivers

The main pressure comes from the Fed path. Markets price a hike at the December meeting as the likelier outcome, which keeps real yields elevated and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. That is the clearest macro explanation for the slide from the May high. Partly offsetting this, the market leans toward at least one cut by mid-2027, so the hawkish stance is seen as a detour rather than a permanent regime and gold keeps some medium-term support. The geopolitical signal also leans supportive: the market gives the US-Iran ceasefire a low chance of lasting through year-end, which implies renewed conflict risk and energy-driven inflation worries that normally favor safe havens. No Adam Khoo evidence is attached, so nothing from that side reinforces or contradicts these signals. The picture is a hawkish rates headwind against a geopolitical and eventual-easing tailwind.

## Continue or revert

The call is neutral with low conviction. The evidence-augmented posterior sits slightly below the raw market prior, but both land in the neutral zone, so there is no real conflict between evidence and odds. With no evidence items to push against the market, the small tilt reflects the weak, downward realized trend and the hawkish near-term rates signal. That tilt is too slight to justify calling for further decline, and the pullback is not strong enough to call a reversal. The trend can continue or fade, and the signals do not favor either. The call would shift bullish if December hike odds fell well below the revision threshold, which would ease the yield pressure, or if cut-by-mid-2027 odds fell under their threshold, which would signal a different and more hawkish rate regime. A recovery in ceasefire-hold odds above its threshold would remove the geopolitical bid and tilt the balance toward continued weakness.

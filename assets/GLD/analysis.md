## Performance (6 months)

Gold has had a hard half-year. It peaked in late spring, then slid in a persistent, orderly way to a trough at the very end of October. The drawdown was deep but gradual, not a crash. The bounce off the low has been shallow, and the price still sits nearer the bottom of its range than the top.

The latest month was flat to slightly softer. No clear base has formed yet.

## Drivers

The main headwind is the Fed path. The market treats a hike at the December meeting as the overwhelmingly likely outcome. That keeps real yields firm and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. A hawkish Fed also tends to support the dollar, which is a further drag on gold. This rate channel is the only live signal behind the call.

There is no supporting evidence on file for gold, so the commentary layer neither backs nor challenges the rate story. The main offset is geopolitics. Renewed conflict could revive safe-haven demand and energy-driven inflation worries. That is not currently feeding into Fed expectations, so it does not outweigh the rate channel.

## Continue or revert

The evidence-adjusted view has nothing to work with beyond the market odds, so it echoes them, and there is no conflict between inputs to resolve. I weight the Fed-hike odds because they are the only live input and they carry a clear mechanism for gold. The price history is consistent with that mechanism, but it is not the reason for the call.

The call stays very bearish with high confidence. While a hike remains the base case, pressure from real yields is more likely to persist than fade. The view would be revisited if the Fed-hike odds at the December meeting fell decisively below even. That would ease real-yield pressure and leave room for a rebound from depressed levels. A geopolitical shock large enough to override rate expectations would also warrant a fresh look. Without either, the pricing and the macro mechanism point the same way.

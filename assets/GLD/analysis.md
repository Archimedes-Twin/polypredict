## Performance (6 months)

Gold climbed to a peak around the middle of the window, then rolled over into a long decline. The drawdown from that peak is deep, and the latest month was a sharp acceleration lower rather than a pause. The window closes at its low, with no meaningful bounce, no recovery attempt and no visible base. The shape is a failed peak followed by a slide that has recently picked up speed, leaving the price at the bottom of its range.

## Drivers

The main driver is the Fed path. The market treats a rate hike at the December meeting as close to a certainty. A tightening bias like that points to firm real yields, which raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset like gold. A hawkish Fed also tends to support the dollar, which adds pressure for an asset priced in dollars.

This is the only signal in the file. The read rests on one macro channel, policy tightening, rather than a broad set of drivers. Inflation, labor and geopolitical inputs are not in the data, so I don't lean on them here.

## Continue or revert

The raw market prior is strongly bearish with high confidence, and it agrees with the downtrend. The crowd expects continuation, not a snapback. There is no decayed video evidence to weigh against the prior, so nothing pulls the other way. The posterior simply matches the prior, and the very bearish rating follows from that agreement.

The judgment is continuation lower. The trend, the market pricing and the hawkish Fed backdrop all point the same direction. The main risk is a hawkish repricing that reverses. If the market retreats meaningfully from pricing a December hike, falling to roughly even odds or below, the bearish case loses its anchor and a reversion higher becomes credible. Because the price is already at its low after an accelerating drop, a sharp but short-lived bounce could occur before the downtrend resumes. Absent that repricing, the balance favors further weakness.

## Performance (6 months)

Gold rose to a peak around the middle of the window, then rolled over into a long, mostly orderly decline. The drawdown is deep, and the latest month was a sharp acceleration lower rather than a pause. The window closes at its low, so there has been no meaningful bounce, no recovery attempt and no visible base. The path is a failed peak followed by a grinding slide that has recently picked up speed. The price sits at the bottom of its range.

## Drivers

The main headwind is the Fed path. The market treats a hike at the year-end meeting as near-certain. That points to firm real yields, which raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset like gold. A hawkish Fed also tends to support the dollar, which adds pressure.

The second signal, a Fed cut by mid-next-year, is close to a coin flip. It suggests the market sees tightening now and possible easing later, but that is little help for near-term gold. Inflation worries and geopolitical stress could draw haven demand, but neither is currently changing the rate expectations that matter most here.

## Continue or revert

Continue. The call stays very bearish. No video evidence is on file, so there is no conflict between prior and evidence to resolve, and the market-implied read carries the call. It is also consistent with the trend, since the crowd expects the decline to persist. The hike signal is decisive and works through real yields and the dollar. The undecided cut signal is a mild offset, which is why conviction is medium rather than high.

The call would revert if hike odds fell far enough that a year-end hike stopped being the base case, because that would remove the main rate headwind. Cut-by-mid-next-year odds have their own invalidation line, but a loss of belief in easing would reinforce the bearish case. A sharp decline from here that does not recover would also show the market had already priced in most of the rate pressure.

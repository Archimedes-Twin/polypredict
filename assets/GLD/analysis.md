## Performance (6 months)

Gold has had a hard six months. It peaked in mid-spring and then slid in an orderly, persistent way into a trough at the very end of October, a deep drawdown rather than a single crash. Since that low the recovery has been shallow, and the price sits closer to the bottom of the range than the top. The latest month has been flat to slightly softer, so the direction is still down and no clear base has formed.

## Drivers

The dominant headwind is the Fed path. The market treats a hike at the December 2026 meeting as the likelier outcome, which keeps real yields firm and raises the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset. That is the clearest macro explanation for the slide from the spring high, and it is the single signal driving the call. No recent Adam Khoo evidence is on file for gold, so nothing in the commentary layer either reinforces or challenges the rate-driven story. The offsetting force is geopolitics, where renewed conflict could revive safe-haven demand and energy-led inflation worries, but that risk is not what the market is currently pricing into the Fed path.

## Continue or revert

The evidence-augmented posterior matches the raw market prior exactly, because there is no evidence to pull the view away from the odds. The crowd expects continuation and the six-month trend agrees, so there is no conflict to resolve. The call is very bearish with high confidence: the slide is more likely to continue than revert while a December hike remains the base case. The view would change if hike odds fell well below the midpoint, since that would remove the main pressure from real yields and open the door to a rebound from depressed levels. A sharp geopolitical shock that overrides rate expectations would be a second reason to reassess. Absent either, the downtrend and the pricing point the same way.

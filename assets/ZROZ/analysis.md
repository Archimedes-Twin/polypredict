## Performance (6 months)

The long-duration Treasury fund has spent the half-year in a broad downtrend. It slid from a summer high into an early-autumn trough. That drawdown was deep enough to count as a real stress episode for long bonds. Buyers then stepped in and recovered a meaningful part of the loss. The most recent stretch has been flat, as if the rebound ran out of momentum. The fund still sits well below its high, so the shape is a long grind lower, a partial bounce, and then a stall. It is not a clean reversal.

## Drivers

Inflation and the long-end yield regime are the main drivers. The September video framework anchors long yields to inflation plus real growth. On that logic, sticky inflation pushes long yields higher whatever the Fed does at the short end, and that weighs on 20+ year bond prices. The framework comes from a single analyst and the evidence is aging, so it should not be over-weighted. The counterweight is the Fed path. Markets price a cut by mid-2027 as slightly better than a coin flip. That is not a strong easing impulse, but it does stop the policy backdrop from turning outright hawkish. The video's point that elevated yields are historically normal is aimed at equities and does little to protect long-bond prices. Labor, geopolitics and sector-specific channels are not flagged as active, so inflation and the rate-path debate carry the story.

## Continue or revert

The call is neutral with low confidence. The market prior leans mildly constructive, because cut odds sit a little above even. The video evidence leans bearish, arguing for an inflation-driven grind higher in long yields. The two partly cancel. The video pulled the posterior down from the prior to roughly a coin flip, but it is one decaying argument about where long yields settle. It is not a near-term catalyst, so it does not justify a downtrend call. The cut market reflects broad pricing of the policy path, so I weight it a bit more. Inflation remains a real headwind, and the stalled rebound fits a market without conviction in either direction. The stated invalidation is that the call turns bearish if cut-by-July-2027 odds fall clearly below the threshold in the signal. That would remove the support the market prior currently gives and leave the inflation-anchored yield argument unopposed.

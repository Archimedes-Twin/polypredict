## Performance (6 months)

Over the last six months the long-duration Treasury fund slid in a clear downtrend, from a June peak to a trough in early October, a drawdown deep enough to mark a real stress episode for long bonds. The most recent month brought a sharp rebound off that low, but it has recovered only part of the decline, and the fund remains well below its high. The shape is a prolonged grind lower followed by a sharp bounce, not a clean reversal.

## Drivers

Inflation is the central driver. The Khoo framework from mid-September holds that long yields are anchored to inflation plus real growth. Inflation near the mid-three range plus modest real growth points toward a long-rate level moving higher, and the Fed controls only the short end. In a hot-inflation regime, long yields drift up whatever the policy path, which weighs on long-duration bond prices. That is one analyst's model, not settled fact, and the evidence is a single item. The market side offers only a partial counterweight. The Polymarket signal on a Fed cut by July of next year sits near a coin flip, so it does not support a confident easing-driven rally in the long end. Cuts would help the front of the curve, but they would not remove the inflation-anchored pressure on long yields. Geopolitics and energy are not flagged as active drivers in the signals, so inflation expectations and the cut path carry the story.

## Continue or revert

The call is neutral with low confidence. The raw market prior is also neutral, and the evidence-augmented posterior sits slightly below it. The inflation-anchored upward drift in long yields tilts the odds a little toward the downside, but that tilt is too small and rests on too little evidence to break the neutral zone. The two views conflict only mildly. The odds say cuts are a toss-up, and the evidence says that even with cuts, long yields face upward pressure. So the recent rebound should not be read as a durable reversal, and the prior downtrend should not be extrapolated either. Neither continuation nor reversion has a clear edge. The call would shift if the cut-by-July-of-next-year odds fell below the stated invalidation level, which would signal a hawkish repricing and push the view toward a bearish call. A clear break in inflation momentum, weakening the case for a rising long-rate anchor, would argue for a more constructive stance on long Treasuries.

## Performance (6 months)

The long-duration Treasury fund has been in a clear downtrend over the half-year. It slid from a early-summer high to an autumn trough, a drawdown deep enough to count as a real stress episode for long bonds. The latest stretch brought a sharp rebound off that low, but it retraced only part of the loss, and the fund still sits well below its high. The overall shape is a prolonged grind lower followed by a sharp but incomplete bounce, not a clean reversal.

## Drivers

Inflation is the main driver. The September video framework says long yields anchor to inflation plus real growth. With inflation running hot, long yields are pushed higher whatever the Fed does at the short end, and that pressure weighs on long-duration bond prices. This is one analyst's model, and the evidence is a single item that is aging. The Fed-cut market is the counterweight. Its odds sit near a coin flip, so it gives no strong easing impulse for the long end. Energy, credit and tech-demand channels are not flagged as active here, so inflation and the policy path carry the story.

## Continue or revert

The call is neutral with low confidence. The video points down and the odds point to no clear direction, so they conflict. I lean toward the market reading. The video rests on a single, time-decayed argument about where long yields settle, not on a catalyst. The cut odds, by contrast, reflect broad pricing of the policy path. Inflation pressure is a real headwind, but it is too thin to override a toss-up market. So the recent bounce is not a confirmed reversal, and the prior slide should not be extrapolated. If cut-by-July odds fell below the stated invalidation threshold, the view would shift bearish.

## Performance (6 months)

The long-duration Treasury fund has been in a clear downtrend over the half-year. It slid from an early-summer high to an autumn trough, a drawdown deep enough to count as a genuine stress episode for long bonds. The latest stretch brought a sharp rebound off that low. The rebound retraced only part of the loss, and the fund still sits well below its high. The overall shape is a prolonged grind lower followed by a sharp but incomplete bounce, not a clean reversal.

## Drivers

Inflation is the main driver. The September video framework anchors long yields to inflation plus real growth. On that logic, hot inflation pushes long yields higher whatever the Fed does at the short end, which weighs on long-duration bond prices. This is one analyst's model, and the evidence is a single aging item. The Fed-cut market is the counterweight. Its odds sit near a coin flip, so it offers no strong easing impulse for the long end. Energy, credit and tech-demand channels are not flagged as active, so inflation and the policy path carry the story.

## Continue or revert

The call is neutral with low confidence. The video points down while the cut odds point to no clear direction, so the two conflict. I lean toward the market reading. The video offers one decaying argument about where long yields settle, not a near-term catalyst. The cut market reflects broad pricing of the policy path. The video did pull the posterior slightly below the market prior, but not far enough to call a downtrend. Inflation is a real headwind. If cut-by-next-summer odds fell below the stated invalidation threshold, the call would revise toward bearish.

PERFORMANCE (6 MONTHS)

Over the six months QQQ ended modestly lower, but the path was a wide round trip rather than a steady slide. It bottomed in late summer, rallied to a window high in early October, then gave back the gains in a sharp, fast unwind through early November. The latest month did nearly all the damage: a steep drawdown that put the fund at its low of the window on the final observation, close to the lows with almost no bounce. The shape is a failed breakout followed by a rapid liquidation, and the trend reads as down.

DRIVERS

Rates are the main macro driver. Futures price a high likelihood of a Fed hike by the December 2026 meeting, which is a hawkish turn that pressures the long-duration growth multiples dominating the index. That signal would be revised if hike odds fell well below the midpoint. Policy and geopolitics add a second layer: Khoo attributes the one-day Nasdaq rout to Democratic rhetoric on an AI and data-center moratorium. He calls it a sentiment shock and argues the Senate math (a filibuster-proof threshold, a presidential veto, and Texas's pledge to finish permitted projects) makes passage unlikely. Earnings and fundamentals are the main offset. He reads hyperscaler negative free cash flow as growth capex and one-offs such as the EU fine and TPU inventory, not weaker operating cash flow, with revenue growth still solid. Against that, his own early-November notes describe AI names far off their highs, rotation into financials, healthcare and staples, and crowded hedge-fund longs being cut. That is a positioning unwind that can keep pushing the index lower in the short run even with earnings intact. His valuation and boom-duration comparisons to the dotcom era support the long run but say little about the near term.

CONTINUE OR REVERT

The call is bearish, but less bearish than the raw market prior, which sits at very bearish. The odds are anchored on a Fed that may hike, and that fits the trend: the crowd expects continuation of the slide. The evidence pulls the other way. Khoo's reading is that this is a policy and sentiment shock with intact earnings, which historically has mean-reverted fast, and the Senate arithmetic argues against a real capex cut. We do not discard the odds, because the hike pricing is a rate headwind that a sentiment rebound does not remove, and the crowding unwind he describes is still running. So the posterior keeps a bearish lean with high confidence, tempered toward a rebound possibility. Continuation of weakness is the base case, with the rebound argument capping how far we lean. The call would soften toward neutral or bullish if hike odds for December fell clearly below the revision threshold, or if the moratorium threat visibly faded and hyperscaler capex guidance held. It would deepen if the hike pricing firmed further, the rotation out of AI broadened, or the fund broke below its November low with weak follow-through on earnings.

PERFORMANCE (6 MONTHS)

Over the last half-year SPY has moved sideways inside a broad range rather than trending. It bottomed in early summer, climbed steadily to a peak in early October, then gave back part of that advance in a sharp, short pullback over the most recent month. The drawdown from the high is modest and well short of a deep correction. The index still sits in the upper part of its range with only a small net gain for the half-year. The record shows a choppy base with a late-stage dip, not a breakdown.

DRIVERS

The main driver is the earnings cycle. Profits for the latest quarter beat an already high bar, growth is broadening beyond the mega-cap technology names, and margins are at a high level that the evidence attributes to real AI cost savings. Earnings have outgrown price, so the forward multiple has slipped below its long-run averages and is well under where it stood at the start of the year. Seasonality adds support, since post-midterm periods have historically been strong once election uncertainty clears. Positioning is also a factor. Hedge funds just made one of their largest de-grossing moves in a decade, and past episodes of that size have clustered near index lows. The offsetting risk is that forced selling from leveraged funds and margin-stretched retail could push prices lower in the very short run, and the evidence frames this as a possibility, not a certainty. Geopolitics is the main headwind in the market odds. The proxy bet on the US-Iran ceasefire holding is priced as more likely to fail than to hold, which raises the risk of an energy and inflation shock that would complicate the Fed's path and pressure equities.

CONTINUE OR REVERT

The posterior leans bullish with low confidence, while the raw market prior leans bearish. The conflict comes from the sources. The prior is driven largely by a pessimistic read on the Iran ceasefire, a single geopolitical proxy that captures tail risk but says little about the earnings base. The evidence is broader and more fundamental: strong and broadening profits, a cheaper multiple, a supportive post-midterm seasonal, and a de-grossing washout that has historically marked lows. It also contrasts this selloff with past policy-driven shocks that recovered quickly, as opposed to earnings-led bear markets that took years. On that balance, continuation of the upward bias is favored over a further slide, and the recent pullback looks more like a reversion toward the range's firmer footing than the start of a new downtrend. Confidence stays low because the odds are real and mechanical deleveraging can still do near-term damage. If ceasefire-hold odds recovered to a clear majority, the geopolitical drag in the prior would fade and the conflict would largely resolve in favor of the bullish view. What would break the call is earnings momentum cracking, forced selling spreading beyond the funds already stressed, or an Iran breakdown that sends energy sharply higher and revives inflation and rate-hike fears.
